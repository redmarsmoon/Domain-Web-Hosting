The build price is rarely the number that matters. What counts is the monthly cost afterwards.

What is AI cost analysis?

AI cost analysis establishes the total cost of owning an AI system: the build, the licences, the per-use charges that grow with adoption, the hosting, the monitoring and the staff time to maintain it, compared against what the task costs you today by hand.

AI carries a cost shape most businesses have not budgeted for before. A great deal of it is charged per use, so the more customers you serve the larger the bill becomes. A quiet month is cheap and a busy month is not.

Add licences, hosting, monitoring and the staff hours to keep it working, and the annual figure frequently bears no resemblance to the original quote.

The comparison that decides everything is against your current manual cost. Without that number you cannot tell whether automation is genuinely cheaper or merely newer, and most proposals never establish it.

Problems this solves

Cost work usually starts when a bill arrives larger than expected, or when finance refuses to sign something they cannot break down.

Frequently asked questions about AI cost analysis

What does AI cost analysis cover?

The full cost of owning a system: build, licences, per-use charges, hosting, monitoring and maintenance, compared against your current manual cost.

Why do AI costs rise when the system works well?

Most AI is charged per use. Serving more customers means more usage, so cost grows with success in a way fixed-licence software does not.

Can the cost be capped?

Usually. Usage limits, caching repeated answers, routing simple work to cheaper models, or self-hosting once volume justifies it. Best designed in early.

What is a token and why does it matter?

The unit most language model providers bill by, roughly a fragment of a word. It matters because it is what your invoice is calculated from.

How do you compare quotes from different suppliers?

By restating them onto identical scope and inclusions first. The lower headline figure is frequently not the cheaper option.

Why measure the manual process?

Because without it you cannot say whether automation is cheaper. Most proposals never establish this number.

What is self-hosting and when is it worth it?

Running the models on infrastructure you control. It becomes cheaper past a certain volume, and we calculate where that point sits for you.

Do you model what happens if volume doubles?

Yes. Sensitivity analysis is part of the work because volume growth is the most common cause of budget failure.

What if provider pricing changes?

We model that too. Pricing has moved repeatedly, usually downward, but assuming it will is not a plan.

How long does the analysis take?

Two to three weeks, mostly spent establishing real volumes and costing the current manual process.

Will you assess a proposal from another supplier?

Yes, frequently. If our own work is among the options, that is declared at the top of the report.

What is caching in this context?

Reusing a previously generated answer rather than paying to produce the same result again. It can substantially reduce recurring cost.

What is model routing?

Sending straightforward requests to a cheaper model and reserving the expensive one for genuinely difficult work.

Do hidden costs usually appear?

Monitoring and staff maintenance time are the two most commonly omitted, and together they are rarely trivial.

Can you analyse a system already running?

Yes, and the numbers are better because actual consumption is visible rather than projected.

What if the analysis says do not automate?

Then that is the finding. At low volumes the manual process is often genuinely cheaper, and we have written that conclusion before.

Who needs to take part in a cost review?

Whoever holds the budget and whoever knows the real volumes, usually finance and operations together.

Does this include implementation cost from us?

Only if we are being considered for the work, and then it is presented alongside the alternatives with our interest declared.

How accurate are the projections?

They carry stated confidence. Volumes measured from your records are firm; provider pricing changes are explicitly uncertain.

What does the analysis itself cost?

It depends on how many options and systems are in scope. The figure is fixed before we start.

Glossary of terms

Cost vocabulary as it applies to AI systems specifically.

Total cost of ownership
Build cost plus everything required to keep a system running, including licences, usage and maintenance.
Per-use pricing
Charging by unit of consumption, so the bill rises directly with adoption rather than staying fixed.
Token
The unit most language model providers bill by, roughly corresponding to a fragment of a word.
Caching
Reusing a previous result instead of paying to generate the same answer again.
Model routing
Sending simpler requests to a cheaper model and only difficult ones to an expensive one.
Baseline cost
What the task costs you today under the current manual process, measured in staff hours.
