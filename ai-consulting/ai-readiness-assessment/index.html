We check whether your business is ready for AI before you spend money on it.

What is ai readiness assessment?

An AI readiness assessment is a structured review of your data, systems and working practices that establishes which tasks can be automated now, which need groundwork first, and which should be left alone. It produces a written verdict, not a sales proposal.

We look at three things. Where your data is kept and what state it is in. Which systems you use and whether they can be connected. How work actually gets done day to day, as opposed to how the manual says it is done.

Most problems we find are not AI problems. Records sit in spreadsheets, two systems do not talk to each other, or a process exists only in one person head. Automating any of that as it stands makes the mess run faster.

We interview the people doing the work, not only managers, because the documented process and the real one usually differ. That gap is where the time goes and where automation either pays or fails.

Frequently asked questions about ai readiness assessment

What is an AI readiness assessment?

A structured review of your data, systems and processes that establishes what AI can realistically do for your business now, what needs fixing first, and what is not worth automating.

How long does an assessment take?

Two to three weeks for a small or medium business, including interviews. Larger organisations with more systems take longer.

What does it cost?

It depends on how many systems and departments are in scope. We quote after a free call and the figure is fixed before we start.

What if we are not ready?

Then we tell you and say what to fix first. Finding out now is far cheaper than finding out halfway through a build.

Do we need technical staff to take part?

No. We work with owners and managers. The technical detail is ours to handle.

Who do you interview?

The people who actually perform the work, plus whoever owns the process. Management alone gives an incomplete picture.

Will this disrupt daily operations?

Very little. Interviews are short and scheduled around your work. We do not need to stop anything.

What do we receive at the end?

A written report with an inventory of systems and data, a ranked task list, required groundwork, costs, and what we advise against.

Can our board question the findings?

That is the intention. Assumptions are stated explicitly so finance and directors can challenge them.

Do you recommend your own build work?

Where a build makes sense we say so, and we say plainly that we are a candidate. You are free to take the report elsewhere.

Is the report useful without us hiring you?

Yes. It is written to stand alone and can be delivered by your own team or another supplier.

What if our data is messy?

Most data is. We measure how messy and tell you what has to be cleaned before anything can be built on it.

Do you assess systems we plan to replace?

We take planned changes into account, since building against a system you are about to retire wastes money.

How do you rank tasks?

By volume, how rule-driven the task is, what it costs you today, and the effort required to automate it.

What is the most common finding?

That the bottleneck is a missing integration or an undocumented process rather than anything requiring AI.

Do you work outside Nairobi?

Yes. Assessments run remotely where practical and we travel for on-site work across Kenya and the region.

Can you assess a single department?

Yes. A single department is often the sensible starting scope, particularly finance, HR or customer service.

Is our information kept confidential?

Yes. What we see stays with us and is never used to train public AI models.

What happens after the assessment?

You decide. If you proceed, the ranked list becomes the roadmap. If you do not, you still know where you stand.

How is this different from a proposal?

A proposal argues for work. An assessment reports what is true, including when the answer is that you should do nothing.

Glossary of terms

Plain definitions of the terms used on this page.

AI readiness
Whether your data, systems and processes can support automation today, without further groundwork.
Baseline
A recorded measure of how long a task takes or how many are handled now, used later to check whether anything improved.
Data quality
How complete, consistent and duplicate-free your records are. Poor data quality is the most common blocker.
Integration
A connection that lets two systems exchange data automatically instead of someone retyping it.
Total cost of ownership
Build cost plus everything it costs to keep running, including licences, usage charges and maintenance.
Process map
A written description of how work actually flows, step by step, including waiting time.
