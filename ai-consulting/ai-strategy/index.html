AI Strategy

A plan for where AI will actually help your business, and where it will not.

Book a free strategy call Call 0723658888

What is AI strategy?

An AI strategy is a written document linking AI spending to specific commercial outcomes. It sets the order of work, states expected cost and return, defines how success will be measured, and names what the business will deliberately not do.

We start from your trading position rather than the technology. Which jobs cost more than they earn. Which orders you turn away because capacity runs out. Which complaint arrives every single week.

AI earns its place only where it shifts one of those. Everything else is activity that looks like progress, and it is usually where the budget quietly goes.

What you receive is a document your managers can act on and your accountant can pull apart. It names what to build, in what order, at what price, and what has to be true for the return to appear.

Why we write strategies we can be judged on

Duara writes AI strategies for Kenyan companies from our base in Nairobi. We sit with the people who hold the budget and the people who run the operation, because a plan agreed by only one of those groups does not survive its first quarter.

We write strategies we would be willing to be judged against later. That is why every recommendation carries a number you can check afterwards, and why the document names the things we think you should refuse.

Problems this solves

Strategy work usually starts because one of these has already happened, and the cost of the next mistake is now visible.

  • error Activity without a stated goal. A competitor announces something, the board asks what you are doing, and work begins before anyone agrees what it is meant to achieve.
  • error Pilots that stall before production. A demonstration impresses everyone and then quietly dies, because nobody defined what moving it into daily use would require.
  • error Departments competing for one budget. Three proposals arrive with no shared basis for comparing them, so the loudest sponsor usually wins.
  • error No agreed measure of success. Nothing was recorded beforehand, so twelve months later nobody can say whether the money achieved anything.

Features

  • tune Commercial framing before technical framing. Every recommendation attaches to a margin, a capacity ceiling or a complaint you already receive.
  • tune A written refusal list. We name what not to do. Clients often tell us this is the section that saved them the most money.
  • tune Justified sequencing. The order of work carries its reasoning, so changing priority later is a decision rather than a drift.
  • tune Measures agreed in advance. What gets measured, and against which baseline, is settled before the first build rather than argued afterwards.
  • tune Running costs modelled at real volume. Usage-priced AI costs more when it succeeds. The numbers reflect busy months, not pilot months.

Benefits

  • trending_up One direction instead of several. Departments stop advancing separate proposals that quietly contradict each other.
  • trending_up Board decisions get faster. Directors approve or reject against stated assumptions rather than debating in the abstract.
  • trending_up Early work funds later work. Sequencing by return means the programme pays for its own next stage.
  • trending_up The result is provable. Because measures were fixed upfront, the outcome is checkable rather than a matter of opinion.

Technologies we use

  • memory Your financial and operational records. The strategy is built from your figures, not from industry averages that may not resemble your business.
  • memory Opportunity scoring. A consistent comparison method so the ranking survives scrutiny from whoever did not get funded.
  • memory Usage-based cost modelling. Projecting the monthly bill at genuine volume, which is where most AI budgets are underestimated.
  • memory Benchmark review. What comparable operations achieve, used as a sanity check rather than as a promise.
  • memory Roadmap handover format. The strategy is written to feed directly into a sequenced delivery plan without rewriting.

Strategy work is analysis rather than engineering. The only tools involved are the ones needed to interrogate your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions about AI strategy

What is an AI strategy?

A written plan linking AI investment to specific commercial outcomes, with the order of work, expected costs, agreed success measures and an explicit list of what not to do.

How does it differ from a readiness assessment?

A readiness assessment establishes what is possible with your current data and systems. A strategy decides what is worth doing and in what order.

We already have a strategy document. Will you review it?

Yes, and reviewing is quicker and cheaper than starting again. The usual gap is that nothing in it connects to a figure the business already tracks.

How long does a strategy take to produce?

Three to four weeks, including two or three working sessions with your leadership team.

Must we use Duara to deliver it?

No. The document is written to be delivered by your own team or any other supplier, and it says so.

Who needs to be in the room?

Whoever controls the budget and whoever runs the affected operation. If either is missing the plan tends not to survive.

What if the conclusion is that AI is not the answer?

Then the strategy says that. Plenty of problems are solved far more cheaply by fixing a process or buying ordinary software.

How far ahead should a strategy look?

Twelve to eighteen months. Past that, both the technology and your own priorities move enough to make the detail fictional.

Are costs included?

Yes, build cost and running cost for each recommendation, modelled at realistic volume rather than pilot volume.

How do you decide what to exclude?

Anything where the expected return fails to clear the full cost, or where the downside risk outweighs the upside.

Can a strategy cover several departments?

Yes, and it usually should, because the strongest opportunities tend to sit in the gaps between departments.

What happens when priorities change?

The strategy carries decision points. Changing direction at one is ordinary; discovering the problem too late is what costs money.

Will it name specific suppliers or products?

Where it matters, with reasoning shown. We also state on the page when we would be a candidate for that work.

Is this worthwhile for a small business?

Yes, though the document is shorter. A small business usually needs one clear priority rather than a portfolio of them.

Do you run workshops as part of it?

Yes, normally two or three sessions to test assumptions with your team before anything is written.

How do you handle internal disagreement?

We write down the competing positions and the evidence for each, so the decision gets made openly rather than by seniority.

Does it look at what competitors are doing?

As a sanity check only. Copying a competitor without knowing their economics is not a strategy, it is a guess.

What form does the deliverable take?

A written document plus a short board paper. No jargon, and no slides that say nothing.

Can you help implement afterwards?

Yes, and we declare that interest inside the document itself so the recommendation can be judged accordingly.

What is the most common mistake you see?

Beginning with the most technically interesting project, so nothing visible exists by the time the budget is next reviewed.

Glossary of terms

The vocabulary used in strategy documents, without the consulting jargon.

AI strategy
A written plan connecting AI investment to commercial outcomes, including what the business will not do.
Commercial outcome
A measurable business result such as lower cost per case, higher conversion, or capacity to take more work.
Pilot
A small trial used to test whether an approach works before committing to full deployment.
Production
A system in genuine daily use by staff or customers, as distinct from a demonstration.
Usage-based pricing
Charging per unit of use, so the bill rises as adoption grows. Standard for most AI services.
Refusal list
The explicit set of proposals the strategy recommends declining, with the reasoning attached.
