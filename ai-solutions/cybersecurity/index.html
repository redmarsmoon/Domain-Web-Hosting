AI and Cybersecurity

Useful for alert volume. Useless if the basics are missing.

Book a free strategy call Call 0723658888

What is cybersecurity?

AI in cybersecurity means using automation to handle alert volume and to recognise increasingly convincing social engineering, applied only once the fundamental controls that prevent most incidents are actually in place.

Most breaches in businesses of this size do not involve sophisticated attacks. They involve a shared password, a former employee whose access was never removed, or somebody being convinced to make a payment.

AI does nothing about any of those. Multi factor authentication, an offboarding checklist and a payment verification step do, and they cost very little compared with any detection tooling.

Where AI does help is genuine: making sense of alert volume that exceeds human review, and recognising phishing that is now well written enough to defeat the training most staff received.

What we require before selling you tooling

Duara advises on the AI aspects of security in Kenya, and routinely tells clients to spend the money on fundamentals instead.

We will not sell detection tooling to an organisation without multi factor authentication. Buying sophisticated monitoring while leaving the front door open is a common and expensive sequence, and it is one a supplier benefits from.

Frequently asked questions about cybersecurity

Does AI improve our security?

For alert volume and convincing phishing, genuinely. For the incidents that actually affect businesses this size, very little.

How do most breaches here happen?

A shared password, a leaver whose access was never removed, or somebody convinced to make a payment. AI addresses none of those.

So what should we spend on first?

Multi factor authentication, an offboarding checklist and a payment verification step. They cost very little and prevent most incidents.

Would you sell us detection tooling anyway?

No. Buying detection while the front door is open is a common and expensive sequence, and a supplier benefits from it.

Where does AI genuinely help?

Triaging alert volume that exceeds human review, and recognising phishing that is now well written enough to defeat older training.

Why is phishing training different now?

Because attempts read properly and are specific to the recipient. Staff trained on bad grammar examples have false confidence rather than preparation.

What is the most expensive common incident?

A payment redirected after somebody was convinced to change bank details. A verification step through a separate channel prevents it and costs nothing.

Are you a security company?

No, and we say so. We advise on the AI aspects and refer you to specialists for the rest rather than claiming coverage we do not have.

Can AI detect insider threats?

It can flag unusual patterns, with all the caution that applies to flagging people. A flag about a colleague is not a finding.

Does alert triage lose genuine alerts?

Any prioritisation risks it, which is why we ask first whether the alerts are worth generating rather than only how to sort them.

How long does the fundamentals review take?

About two weeks, and it frequently concludes that no AI tooling should be bought yet.

What about AI generated attacks?

They are real and mostly make existing attack types more convincing rather than creating new ones, which is why training matters more than tooling.

Should we worry about deepfake voice calls?

For payment authorisation specifically, yes. A verification step through a known channel addresses it better than any detection tool.

Does using AI tools create new risk?

Yes, mainly through staff pasting confidential material into consumer tools. That is a policy and sanctioned tool question rather than a detection one.

Can it monitor our staff?

We would decline building continuous staff monitoring. It creates its own risks and the incident pattern does not justify it.

Will this satisfy our auditors?

Fundamentals in place and documented usually satisfies more than tooling does, and we would suggest showing them that first.

Do we need a security operations centre?

Rarely at this scale. It is an expensive answer to a problem most Kenyan businesses of this size do not yet have.

What about ransomware?

Backups you have actually tested restoring matter more than detection. An untested backup is a plan rather than a protection.

Who sees our security assessment?

Only your own people and ours working on it. Findings about weaknesses are sensitive and stay tightly held.

What does security advice cost?

The fundamentals review is short and quoted upfront, and it frequently ends in a recommendation to spend elsewhere.

Glossary of terms

Security terms in plain language.

Multi factor authentication
Requiring more than a password, which prevents a large share of incidents at very low cost.
Offboarding
Removing access when somebody leaves, frequently incomplete and a common cause of incidents.
Payment verification
Confirming a payment change through a separate channel, which prevents the most costly common fraud.
Alert fatigue
The point at which security alerts are no longer read because there are too many.
Social engineering
Convincing a person to act rather than defeating a technical control.
Phishing simulation
Testing staff with realistic fake attempts to see who would fall for them.
