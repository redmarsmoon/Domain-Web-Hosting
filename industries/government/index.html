AI for Government in Kenya

Public services where a citizen cannot simply take their business elsewhere.

Book a free strategy call Call 0723658888

What is government?

AI for government covers citizen enquiry handling, records digitisation, procurement administration and reporting for national and county entities, applied where the people served have no alternative provider and no ability to opt out.

The defining feature of public service delivery is that the citizen cannot go elsewhere. A bank customer treated badly moves their account; somebody who needs a permit cannot.

That changes the standard. A system that works well for eighty percent of people and fails the rest is acceptable in a competitive market and is not acceptable when the failed twenty percent have nowhere else to go.

It also means the fallback matters more than the automation. Every service needs a route that works for somebody without a smartphone, without data, and without literacy in the language the system defaults to.

Why every service keeps a non digital route

Duara works with county governments, national agencies and public sector suppliers from Nairobi, on service administration rather than on policy.

We insist on a working non digital route for every service. Digitising a public service without one excludes the citizens who most need it, and the exclusion is invisible in the usage statistics because excluded people simply do not appear in them.

Problems this solves

These are service delivery and administrative problems, and they fall hardest on the people with the fewest alternatives.

  • error Enquiries beyond capacity. Citizens ask the same questions in volume and the office cannot answer them all, so people queue physically instead.
  • error Paper records that cannot be searched. Files exist and finding one requires somebody physically looking, which delays every dependent decision.
  • error Procurement administration. Tender processes are document heavy and contested, and administrative errors create genuine disputes.
  • error Reporting consuming staff. Statutory and oversight reporting is rebuilt each period from records not organised for it.

Frequently asked questions about government

Why insist on a non digital route?

Because citizens cannot go elsewhere. Digitising without a fallback excludes the people who most need the service.

Would we not see that in the statistics?

No, and that is the problem. Usage figures count the people who got through. The excluded are invisible by construction.

Can it decide who qualifies for a service?

No. Eligibility stays a human decision that can be explained and appealed, which matters more in public service than anywhere else.

What is a citizen answer based on?

Only your published requirements, fees and procedures. A government system inventing a requirement causes real harm to somebody who acts on it.

What if our published documents disagree?

We report the contradiction rather than quietly implementing one version, since either choice would be a policy decision we should not make.

Can it handle enquiries in Swahili?

Yes, and in public service that is a requirement rather than a feature, since language is one of the main exclusion barriers.

Does it work over basic phones?

Yes, over voice and SMS, which is the practical minimum for a service intended to reach everybody.

Can it digitise our paper records?

Yes, with originals retained. Searchability usually transforms decision times more than any other single change.

Does it help with procurement?

With document handling, compliance checking and the audit trail. Award decisions stay entirely with your committees.

Will it reduce tender disputes?

Consistent administration and a clear trail remove the errors that generate most challenges. Substantive disputes are a different matter.

How long does a public sector project take?

Eight to sixteen weeks, with the non digital route designed before the digital one.

Can county governments use this?

Yes, and county service delivery is where enquiry volume and records problems are usually most acute.

What about data protection obligations?

Citizen data stays within your systems and we build to that. Your data protection officer confirms compliance for the specific service.

Does it produce oversight reporting?

It assembles returns from records you hold. Submission and accuracy remain your responsibility.

Can it work with our existing systems?

Yes, and we would rather extend what exists than add another system for staff and citizens to learn.

What if staff resist it?

Worth addressing directly. In public service the concern is often about accountability rather than workload, and that needs a different conversation.

Will citizens trust an automated answer?

More readily when a person is clearly reachable and the answer cites the published rule it came from.

Can it handle peak periods?

Yes, and deadline driven peaks are where remote answering most reduces physical queues.

Who can access citizen records?

Only your authorised staff under your own rules. Records are not exported and train no public AI model.

What does a public sector project cost?

Service volume and records condition determine it, quoted after the service review.

Glossary of terms

Public service terms in plain language.

Service delivery
Providing a public service to citizens, measured properly by who is reached rather than by volume.
Digital exclusion
Being unable to access a service because of device, data, literacy or language barriers.
Grounded answering
Responding only from published official information rather than general knowledge.
Audit trail
The contemporaneous record of what was done, which public administration is regularly asked to produce.
Eligibility
Whether somebody qualifies for a service, which remains a human decision.
Records digitisation
Converting paper archives into searchable form while retaining the originals.
