ERP Automation

Fix the manual work around your ERP instead of replacing the ERP.

What is ERP automation?

ERP automation removes the manual steps that have grown up around an existing ERP system, including data entry, approval chasing and the spreadsheets people maintain alongside it, without replacing the ERP itself.

Companies buy an ERP to hold everything in one place, then discover it is awkward for three specific tasks. So people work around it, and a spreadsheet appears beside it.

Within two years the ERP holds the official numbers and the spreadsheets hold the working ones. The system did not fail; the gaps around it were filled by hand.

The instinct at that point is to replace the ERP. That is a large, expensive project with a poor success record, and it is usually the wrong answer. Automating the specific gaps is smaller, cheaper and fixes the actual complaint.

Problems this solves

These are the gaps that appear around an ERP rather than faults inside it.

  • error Spreadsheets beside the system. People maintain their own records because the ERP is awkward for a specific task, and the two versions drift apart.
  • error Data rekeyed into the ERP. Orders and invoices arrive by email and get typed in, which is slow and introduces errors into the system of record.
  • error Approvals chased by email. A purchase or credit approval sits in somebody inbox with no visibility of where it is stuck.
  • error Reports rebuilt by hand. The report management wants is assembled in Excel every month because the ERP does not produce it in that shape.

Frequently asked questions about ERP automation

Should we just replace our ERP?

Usually not. Where the real problem is three awkward tasks and a spreadsheet habit, replacement solves none of it at very high cost.

Do you sell ERP migrations?

No, and we will say when your ERP is not the problem even though the smaller project is worth less to us.

Which ERPs do you work with?

SAP, Oracle, Sage, Odoo and Microsoft Dynamics, plus older systems still running plenty of Kenyan businesses.

What if our ERP has no API?

We integrate at database or file level, and we state the risks of that rather than presenting it as equivalent to a proper interface.

Why do you ask about our spreadsheets?

Because a workaround usually encodes a real requirement. Removing one without understanding why it exists breaks something.

Will it write into the ERP?

Where needed, yes, and always proved in a test environment first. A bad write into a system of record is expensive to unwind.

Can it handle approvals?

Yes, with status visible to the requester, which is usually the actual complaint rather than the approval itself.

Can it produce our monthly reports?

Yes, in the shape management actually asks for, which is normally why they were being rebuilt in Excel.

How long before the workarounds are gone?

Six to ten weeks, starting with the workaround that costs the most time rather than the one easiest to build.

Will it break our ERP support agreement?

Integration through supported interfaces normally does not. We check your agreement before touching anything unusual.

Can we keep using our spreadsheets?

Some, and we connect them rather than banning them. Ignoring them entirely leaves the drift you are trying to fix.

Does this require ERP consultants?

Not usually. This is integration and automation work rather than reconfiguration of the ERP itself.

What about master data problems?

Automation exposes them quickly. Where customer or product records are inconsistent, we report it rather than automating on top.

Will it cover all our plants and depots?

Yes, and multi site operations often have the most workarounds because each site invented its own.

Will this survive an ERP upgrade?

Where we use supported interfaces, largely. We document what was built so an upgrade is a check rather than a rediscovery.

Is any of this AI based?

Mostly not. Reading unstructured invoices uses it, and the rest is rules, which is what you want touching a system of record.

Who owns this afterwards?

You do. It is documented so your own IT team can maintain and extend it.

Can it cut our month end time?

Usually, since reports rebuilt by hand and rekeyed data are the two things that most often delay a close.

Is our ERP data confidential?

Yes. It stays inside your systems, is not copied out, and no public AI model is trained on it.

What does ERP automation cost?

The number of gaps and the state of your ERP interfaces determine it, quoted after the review.

Glossary of terms

ERP terms in plain language.

ERP
The single system intended to hold finance, stock, orders and operations together.
System of record
The authoritative source for a piece of data, which spreadsheets quietly undermine.
Workaround
A manual process people invented because the system was awkward for a specific task.
Master data
The core records such as customers, products and suppliers that everything else references.
Module
A functional part of an ERP, such as finance, stock or purchasing.
Shadow system
A spreadsheet or tool running alongside the ERP and holding the numbers people actually use.
