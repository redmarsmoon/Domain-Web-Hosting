Warehouse Automation

Better picking and location accuracy without buying any equipment.

What is warehouse automation?

Warehouse automation in this sense is the software layer directing work inside a warehouse, including where stock is located, the sequence pickers follow, scanning verification at each step and checking a dispatch before it leaves.

A picker walking the wrong route is the largest hidden cost in most warehouses. It does not appear on any report, and it happens on every order.

The related cost is picking the wrong thing. That surfaces later as a customer complaint, a return and a credit note, by which point it has cost several times what catching it at the shelf would have.

Both are software problems rather than equipment problems. Directing the route and verifying by scan addresses them at a fraction of what conveyors and automated storage cost.

Problems this solves

These are all consequences of the warehouse relying on knowledge held by individual people.

  • error Pickers walking the wrong route. The sequence follows the order sheet rather than the layout, and the wasted walking never appears on any report.
  • error Wrong items picked. The error is found by the customer, when it costs a return, a credit note and a delivery rather than a scan.
  • error Locations held in memory. Experienced staff know where things are, which works until they are on leave or the item was moved.
  • error Dispatch errors leaving the building. Nothing verifies the load against the order, so a short or wrong dispatch is discovered at the customer.

Frequently asked questions about warehouse automation

Do we need conveyors or robots?

Almost certainly not first. Handheld scanning solves most accuracy problems, and where equipment is genuinely justified we will say so.

Why start with locations?

Because automating movement over inaccurate locations gives you expensive equipment moving stock to the wrong place faster.

What does the location audit involve?

Recording and verifying every location. It is unglamorous and it determines whether everything after it works.

How does directed picking help?

It sequences picks by physical route rather than by order entry, removing walking that nobody was measuring.

When are picking errors caught?

At the shelf, by scan, where the cost is seconds rather than a return, a credit note and a redelivery.

Does it check dispatches?

Yes, against the order before the load leaves, which is the last cheap place to find an error.

Will you measure our pickers?

Performance is visible, and we advise using it to find where the process fails rather than to rank individuals.

Why that caution?

Because a warehouse that feels ranked works to the measure rather than to the job, and the data stops describing reality.

What hardware is needed?

Handheld scanners and a network that reaches the racking. We keep the requirement modest deliberately.

How long before picking is directed?

Six to nine weeks, with the location audit first and scanning at pick before routing changes.

Does it work with our existing stock system?

Yes, feeding your ERP or stock platform rather than replacing it.

Can it handle batch and expiry picking?

Yes, directing pickers to the correct batch, which matters most in pharmaceutical and food operations.

What about serial numbers?

Captured at pick and dispatch where you track them, which supports warranty and recall work later.

Can it manage several warehouses?

Yes, with layouts and rules per site, since warehouses are rarely laid out the same way.

Does it help with returns?

Yes, directing putaway of returned stock, which is a common source of location error when done informally.

What if our racking is not labelled?

Labelling comes first. It is a prerequisite rather than an optional extra, and we will say so at the review.

Will temporary staff manage it?

Better than the current arrangement, since directed work removes the dependence on knowing where things are.

Can it show where errors concentrate?

Yes, by product, aisle and shift, and it frequently shows a labelling or layout cause rather than a picker one.

Who can see our warehouse data?

Only your own staff under your permissions. Nothing is exported to us and no public AI model is trained on it.

What does warehouse automation cost?

Warehouse size, scanner count and integration determine it, quoted after the warehouse review.

Glossary of terms

Warehouse terms in plain language.

Putaway
Placing received goods into a storage location, and recording where they went.
Directed picking
The system telling a picker what to collect and in what physical sequence.
Bin location
The specific shelf or position where an item is stored.
Mispick
Collecting the wrong item or quantity, cheapest to catch at the shelf.
Pick path
The route a picker walks to complete an order, and the largest hidden cost in most warehouses.
Dispatch verification
Checking a load against the order before it leaves the building.
