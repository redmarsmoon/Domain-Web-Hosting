What is business automation?

Business automation replaces repetitive manual steps in a process with software that follows fixed rules, after the process itself has been examined and simplified, so the automation is built on something worth keeping.

Most automation projects start from the wrong end. Somebody picks the process that is easiest to demonstrate rather than the one quietly costing the most, and the result is impressive and worth very little.

The second common mistake is automating a process nobody has looked at in years. If three of the seven steps exist because of a system replaced in 2019, automating all seven preserves the waste and makes it permanent.

The order that works is: find where the money actually goes, remove the steps that should not exist, then automate what remains. The third part is usually the easiest of the three.

Why we usually recommend less

Duara automates business processes from Nairobi for organisations where the same work is done by hand every day and nobody has counted what it costs.

We frequently recommend less automation than clients expect. Where two of seven steps can simply be deleted, that is a better outcome than automating all seven, and it is cheaper for you and smaller for us. We would rather say it than sell around it.

Frequently asked questions about business automation

Which process should we automate first?

With measurement. The process that is easiest to demonstrate is rarely the one costing most, and choosing without counting is the usual mistake.

Why simplify before automating?

Because automating waste makes it permanent. If three of seven steps exist for a system replaced years ago, automating all seven preserves them forever.

Will you ever recommend doing less?

Frequently. Where steps can simply be deleted, that is cheaper for you and smaller for us, and we would rather say it than sell around it.

Is any of this actually AI?

Only where a step genuinely needs interpretation. Most business processes have right answers, and rules are cheaper and more predictable for those.

How do we know it worked?

Against the baseline recorded before we built anything. Without that, any improvement claim is an impression rather than evidence.

What if the gain is smaller than expected?

We report it. That is the point of measuring first, and it is more useful to you than a flattering summary.

How long does one process take to automate?

Four to eight weeks, with the first two spent measuring and simplifying rather than building.

What happens to cases the rules cannot handle?

They go to a person with the reason attached. Forcing them through to keep the numbers clean is how automation loses trust.

Can we automate everything?

No, and a high exception rate is a signal the process needs judgement rather than more rules.

Will staff lose their jobs?

That is your decision and we will not pretend otherwise. Most clients redeploy people rather than reduce headcount, but you should decide before starting.

Do we need new systems?

Usually not. Most automation connects what you already run, and we prefer that to a platform migration.

Can our team maintain the rules?

Yes. Rules stay visible and documented, so changing a threshold does not require calling us.

What if our process changes?

Rules are built to be edited. A process that never changes is unusual, and we assume yours will.

Which processes suit this best?

High volume, repetitive, with consistent right answers. Low volume work with lots of judgement rarely justifies the build.

Can you automate across departments?

Yes, and cross department processes are frequently where the largest waste sits, because nobody owns the whole thing.

Do you work with our existing software?

Yes, connecting what you have rather than replacing it, unless replacement is genuinely the cheaper answer.

How disruptive is this?

The measurement phase is not disruptive at all. Changes go live gradually, and we do not switch a whole process over in one step.

What if we tried automation before and it failed?

Usually the process was never simplified, or the wrong one was chosen. Both are worth checking before trying again.

Who sees how our business actually runs?

Only the people working on your project. What we learn about your processes stays with you and trains no public AI model.

What does process automation cost?

Process complexity and system count determine it, quoted after the measurement rather than before it.

Glossary of terms

Process terms in plain language.

Process mapping
Writing down each step of how work actually happens, which often differs from how it is believed to happen.
Cycle time
How long one instance of a process takes from start to finish.
Exception rate
The proportion of cases the rules cannot handle, which sets a practical ceiling on automation.
Straight through processing
A case completing with no human intervention at all.
Rules engine
Software that applies defined logic consistently and can be inspected.
Baseline
The measured cost of a process before any change, used to check whether the change helped.
