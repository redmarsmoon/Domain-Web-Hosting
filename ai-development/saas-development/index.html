What is SaaS development?

SaaS development is building software sold as a subscription, which requires customer data kept properly separate, billing that works without manual intervention, and a cost per customer low enough for the price to make sense.

Building the product is the part founders plan for. The parts that decide whether the business works are the ones underneath: keeping one customer data away from another, billing that runs itself, and knowing what serving a customer actually costs.

AI features make that last point sharper. Traditional software costs almost nothing per extra user; AI features cost real money per request, so a flat monthly price against unlimited AI usage can lose money on your best customers.

Working out the unit economics before launch is unglamorous and it is what separates a product from an expensive hobby.

Duara builds subscription products from Nairobi for founders and for established businesses productising something they already do well internally.

Frequently asked questions about SaaS development

What do founders most often get wrong?

Unit economics. Nobody calculates what serving one customer costs, so the price is a guess and the margin is unknown.

Why do AI features change the pricing question?

Because traditional software costs almost nothing per extra user, while AI costs real money per request. Flat pricing against unlimited usage loses money on heavy users.

Will you tell us if the model does not work?

Yes, before the build rather than after launch. Where the numbers do not work, saying so is more useful than taking the project.

What is tenant isolation?

Keeping each customer data architecturally separate, rather than filtering it in code somebody has to remember to apply.

Why does that need designing in?

Because retrofitting it is expensive and risky, and one customer seeing another data is the failure no good product recovers from.

How do you stop a runaway AI cost?

Spend caps per customer and in aggregate. A runaway feature on a subscription is a loss that scales with your success.

How long to a first release?

Twelve to twenty weeks, with a deliberately narrow product shipping to early customers rather than a complete one shipping late.

Should we charge per user or per usage?

It depends on your cost shape, which is why we calculate that first. Usage based pricing suits AI heavy products more often than founders expect.

Can it handle M-Pesa subscriptions?

Yes, and recurring mobile money collection has real quirks worth designing around rather than discovering.

What about international card payments?

Yes, and most Kenyan SaaS products need both, since your local and export customers pay differently.

Does billing handle failed payments?

Yes, including retries and dunning, because that is where subscription revenue quietly leaks rather than dramatically fails.

Can customers sign up without us?

That is the goal, and it is the difference between a product and a service with a login attached.

Could we run the product without you?

Yes. Code, infrastructure and customer relationships are yours, with no dependency on us to keep it operating.

Can you productise something we do internally?

Often, and it is a good starting point since the method is already proved. The work is generalising it beyond your own case.

How do we know which customers are unprofitable?

Usage metering per customer, which AI costs make necessary rather than optional.

What if we grow faster than expected?

Infrastructure scales, and spend caps keep the AI side from scaling faster than revenue.

Can we start with a single customer?

Yes, and it is often sensible, provided isolation is built in from the start rather than added when the second arrives.

Do you take equity instead of fees?

No. We are a development company rather than an investor, and mixing the two makes our advice less useful to you.

Where does customer data live?

In your own infrastructure, under your control, which also matters to your customers asking the same question.

What does a SaaS build cost?

Scope and integration count determine it, quoted after the product and pricing review.

Glossary of terms

Subscription product terms in plain language.

Multi tenancy
One system serving many customers with their data kept separate.
Unit economics
What it costs to serve one customer, against what that customer pays.
Churn
The rate at which subscribers cancel, which determines whether growth compounds.
Dunning
The process of recovering failed payments, where subscription revenue quietly leaks.
Self serve
A customer signing up and getting value without a person involved.
Usage metering
Recording what each customer consumes, made necessary by per request AI costs.
