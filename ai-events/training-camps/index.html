AI Training Camps and Intensive Programmes

The gap between the days is where the learning happens.

Book a free strategy call Call 0723658888

What is training camps?

A training camp is a multi day AI programme spread over several weeks, where each day is followed by practice on real work, so that competence forms between the sessions rather than during them.

One day gets a team started. It does not make them competent, because nobody becomes competent at anything by watching it done for six hours. Competence forms when a person tries something on their own work and it does not go smoothly.

So the camp is spread out. Three days over three weeks, with practice on real tasks in between, and each session beginning with what went wrong during the gap. That opening conversation is usually the most valuable part of the day.

This makes the format inconvenient on purpose. Running the days back to back is easier to schedule and produces noticeably less, because there is no gap in which anybody discovers what they did not understand.

How the process works, step by step

Camps run three to five days spread over the same number of weeks. The spacing is part of the design rather than a scheduling compromise.

  1. Step 1

    Free scoping conversation

    We establish which team is attending, what they do daily, and whether a camp is genuinely the right format.

  2. Step 2

    Practice time agreed with managers

    Before anything is booked, since a camp without protected gaps becomes three disconnected days.

  3. Step 3

    Material built for the department

    Examples from the work the team actually does rather than a general programme.

  4. Step 4

    Days run with practice between

    Each session opening on the problems people hit while using the tools on real tasks.

  5. Step 5

    Assessment and written record

    What each person can demonstrate at the end, given to you rather than described.

Frequently asked questions about training camps

Why not run the days back to back?

Because the gap is the training. Back to back is easier to schedule and produces noticeably less, since nobody discovers what they did not understand.

What happens during the gaps?

People use the tools on their own work. The difficulties that matter only appear on genuine tasks rather than on exercises.

How much practice time is needed?

A few hours between days is usually enough, provided managers genuinely protect it rather than agreeing to it in principle.

What if managers will not protect it?

Then book a single session instead. A camp without the gaps becomes three disconnected days at several times the cost.

How many days should a camp run?

Three for a team new to this, five where they are already using the tools and need to go considerably further.

Can it be built around one department?

Yes, and it works better that way. Finance and HR need different examples, and a mixed camp approximates for both.

What happens if somebody misses a day?

We cover what they missed at the start of the next one, though missing a gap matters more than missing a session.

Is there an assessment?

Yes, at the end, recording what each person can demonstrate rather than who attended.

Does passing mean they will keep using it?

No, and we do not claim otherwise. Assessment shows capability on the day, and habit depends on the work people return to.

Can a camp run remotely?

Yes, and the days need to be shorter. Attention on a call runs out sooner than attention in a room, whatever the material is.

Can it be hybrid?

Yes, with deliberate handling so remote attendees participate rather than watch, which does not happen by pointing a camera at the room.

How large can a group be?

Small enough that everybody can raise their own work during the openings, since that conversation is where most of the value sits.

Is this the same as a course?

No. A course delivers content, and a camp is built around practice between sessions with content arranged to support it.

Do people need existing AI experience?

For a foundation camp no. For an advanced one yes, and we would say so rather than let a team sit through the wrong programme.

Can you train our trainers instead?

Yes, though it is a different design. Teaching somebody to run this is a separate objective from teaching them to use the tools.

What material do people keep?

The practice tasks, the examples built around their work, and their own assessment record.

Would you recommend against a camp?

Where a team needs awareness rather than competence, yes. Several days for something one session covers is money badly spent.

Can it be spread over more weeks?

Yes, within reason. Too long a gap and people arrive having forgotten the previous day, which defeats the purpose.

Who sees our internal work during practice?

Only the trainers, under whatever confidentiality arrangement you set before the camp begins.

What does a training camp cost?

Days, group size and how much material is built around your department determine it, quoted after the scoping conversation.

Glossary of terms

Camp terms in plain language.

Spacing
Running days weeks apart so practice can happen between them, which is where competence forms.
Practice task
Real work done between sessions, chosen because genuine work produces genuine difficulties.
Problem led opening
Starting a day with what went wrong in the gap rather than with prepared material.
Foundation camp
Three days for a team new to using these tools in daily work.
Advanced camp
Five days for a team already using the tools who need to go considerably further.
Assessment
A demonstration of what somebody can do, which is not the same as a record of who attended.
