AI Workflow Automation

Processes that run themselves instead of needing someone to push them along.

Book a free strategy call Call 0723658888

What is AI workflow automation?

AI workflow automation runs a multi step process end to end, moving work between systems and people automatically, pausing where a human judgement is genuinely required, and stopping visibly with an alert when something goes wrong rather than continuing silently.

Most processes stall not because a step is difficult but because somebody has to remember to do the next one. The work waits, nobody is at fault, and the delay is invisible until a customer asks.

We automate the whole path. A form is submitted, details are validated, records are created, the right person is notified, and the outcome is recorded, without anybody carrying the case between stages.

Where a step needs judgement, the process stops and asks a named person, then continues once they decide. That distinction, between steps that follow rules and steps that need a view, is the design work that matters.

Why a failing workflow stops loudly

Duara builds process automation from Nairobi for businesses where work moves between several people and systems, and where the delay is waiting rather than working.

We insist that a failing workflow stops loudly rather than continuing with a missing step. Silent partial completion is the worst outcome available: the process appears finished, the gap is discovered weeks later, and nobody can say which cases were affected.

Features

  • tune Rules and judgement separated. Steps that follow rules are automated, steps needing a view stop for a person. Getting that line right is most of the design.
  • tune Loud failure. A broken step halts the case and raises an alert rather than skipping onward, because silent partial completion is the worst outcome.
  • tune Live case visibility. Anyone can see where a case sits without asking, which removes most of the internal chasing.
  • tune Context carried through handovers. When work moves to another person, the reasoning from earlier steps travels with it.
  • tune Amendable by your team. Processes are documented and configurable, so a rule change does not require us.

Technologies we use

  • memory Process orchestration. Coordinating steps across systems and people with state tracked centrally rather than inferred.
  • memory System integration. Connecting the applications each step touches so data moves rather than being rekeyed between them.
  • memory Approval routing. Directing judgement steps to the right person with the context they need to decide.
  • memory Failure detection and alerting. Halting on error, raising an alert, and recording exactly which cases were affected.
  • memory Audit logging. A complete record of each case, including who approved what and when.

Failure handling is the part that determines whether a workflow is trustworthy. Anything can run a happy path; the design question is what happens when a step does not complete.

Frequently asked questions about AI workflow automation

What happens when a step fails?

The case halts and an alert is raised. It never continues with a missing step, because silent partial completion is the worst possible outcome.

Why is silent failure so serious?

The process appears finished, the gap surfaces weeks later, and nobody can say which cases were affected. Loud failure avoids all three.

Which steps do you refuse to automate?

Those genuinely needing judgement. We map that line deliberately and leave those steps with a person even where automating them would be technically possible.

Can we change the process later?

Yes, and rules and routing are documented and configurable specifically so you can. A process you cannot amend decays quickly.

How long does one workflow take to build?

Four to six weeks, with mapping taking longer than the build itself.

Why does mapping take so long?

Because the documented process and the real one differ, and building against the documented version fails immediately on contact with actual work.

Does it run alongside the manual process first?

Yes. Parallel running finds gaps while the manual path still exists as a fallback.

Can anyone see where a case has reached?

Yes, which removes most internal chasing. Where is this case becomes a glance rather than several phone calls.

What systems can it connect?

The applications each step touches, through their APIs. Where a system has no interface we say so during mapping.

Is this the same as rules based automation?

It includes it. Rule driven steps are automated conventionally, and AI is used only where a genuine judgement is needed, which is cheaper and more predictable.

Do approvals slow it down?

Far less than the current waiting, because approvals are routed with context and chased automatically rather than sitting unnoticed.

What if an approver is unavailable?

Cover routing is configured during mapping, so absence does not stall a case indefinitely.

Can it handle exceptions?

Yes, and exception routes are designed explicitly rather than left to fall through. Most process failures are unhandled exceptions.

Is every automated step recorded?

A complete one, including who approved what and when, which matters for regulated processes and for disputes.

Can we automate several processes?

Yes, though we recommend one at a time. Proving one properly is faster than half building three.

Which process should we start with?

The one causing the most internal frustration. That is usually where waiting dominates and the return is largest.

Does it work across departments?

Yes, and cross departmental processes are frequently where the greatest delay sits, because handovers are where work waits.

Will staff resist it?

Less than expected, since it removes chasing rather than judgement. We involve them in mapping for that reason.

What if our process changes seasonally?

Variants are supported, and seasonal rule changes can be scheduled rather than reconfigured manually each time.

What does workflow automation cost to run?

Case volume sets the running cost, and we quote it against your own case counts before any build is agreed.

Glossary of terms

Workflow vocabulary, defined plainly.

Workflow
A defined sequence of steps that moves a piece of work from start to completion.
Orchestration
Coordinating steps across different systems and people while tracking overall state.
Happy path
The route a case takes when everything works as intended, which is the easy part to build.
Exception
A case that cannot follow the normal route and needs different handling.
Approval step
A point where the process pauses for a person to decide before continuing.
Audit log
The record of every action taken on a case, including who approved what and when.
