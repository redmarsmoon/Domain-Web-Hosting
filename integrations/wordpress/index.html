WordPress Integration

Your website faces the internet, so treat it as the exposed edge.

What is WordPress?

WordPress integration means connecting your public website to internal systems, designed on the assumption that a public site is the most exposed component you run and should hold as little as possible.

A WordPress site is public by definition. It is scanned continuously, it runs plugins written by many different people, and it is the component of your estate most likely to be compromised at some point.

That should decide what you put on it. A form that writes a lead into a system elsewhere is sensible. A site holding customer records, or credentials that reach your ERP, is putting your most sensitive data behind your least defensible perimeter.

The useful design is one direction. WordPress captures and displays, systems behind it hold and decide, and the connection between them carries the narrowest possible permissions.

Why customer data stays off the site

Duara integrates WordPress sites with internal systems from Nairobi, and treats the site as the exposed edge rather than as an equal partner.

We keep customer data off the website wherever possible. Sites get compromised, plugins carry vulnerabilities nobody knew about, and data held on a public site is data exposed by somebody else mistake rather than yours.

Frequently asked questions about WordPress

Is WordPress secure enough for this?

The question is not whether it can be secured but what a compromise would reach. That is decided by what the site holds and what access it carries.

Should customer data live on the site?

Wherever possible, no. Data on a public site is exposed by somebody else plugin vulnerability rather than by anything you did.

What access should the site have?

The narrowest that works. A site holding keys reaching your ERP turns a website compromise into a business compromise.

What is one direction design?

The site captures and displays while systems behind hold and decide. It contains what a breach can reach rather than assuming there will not be one.

Can form submissions go straight to our CRM?

Yes, and that is the most common request. It also means enquiries are answered sooner because nobody is copying them.

Does anything stay on the site?

As little as possible. Some data has to for the site to function, and we minimise it deliberately rather than by default.

Do you use plugins for this?

Where a maintained one fits, yes. Where the only options are abandoned or poorly maintained, building is safer than installing.

What about the site being updated?

Unmaintained plugins are where most compromises begin, so we give maintenance guidance even though it is not integration work.

How quickly can the site be connected?

One to four weeks, starting with what the site should and should not hold rather than with the connection itself.

Can it publish content from another system?

Yes, from a single source, since maintaining product or service information in two places always ends in divergence.

Will it slow the site down?

No. Work happens outside the page load, and site speed matters for both visitors and search.

Can it handle multilingual content?

Yes, and publishing from one source makes multilingual far easier than maintaining parallel copies by hand.

Is this the same as WooCommerce work?

Related but distinct. WooCommerce adds a store with its own concerns. This is the site and its connection to your systems.

What if our site is already compromised?

That is a security clean up before an integration, and we would say so rather than connecting a site with an unknown problem.

Can our web developer maintain it?

Yes, and it is built on published interfaces and documented so they can.

Do you host the site?

Hosting is separate work and we would keep the two conversations distinct rather than bundling them.

Can it capture enquiries from WhatsApp too?

That is a separate channel with its own rules, and worth connecting alongside since most Kenyan enquiries arrive there rather than on a form.

Will it survive a WordPress update?

Built on published interfaces, largely. Anything relying on plugin internals is what breaks at update time.

Who can access the connection credentials?

They are held on your site with the narrowest permissions, and you can revoke them from the receiving system at any time.

What does WordPress integration cost?

Scope and system count determine it, quoted after the site review.

Glossary of terms

WordPress terms in plain language.

Public surface
The part of your infrastructure reachable from the internet, which is scanned continuously.
Scoped credentials
Access limited to exactly what is needed, so a compromise stays contained.
One direction design
Data moving from the site into systems rather than the site holding it.
REST API
The published WordPress interface, which survives updates better than plugin internals.
Plugin vulnerability
A security flaw in an extension, which is how most WordPress compromises begin.
Single source
Content maintained in one place and published outward, rather than kept in two.
