AWS Integration

Everything is available. That is the problem as well as the appeal.

What is AWS?

AWS integration means building on or connecting to Amazon Web Services, where the practical constraints are cost behaviour, the absence of a local region for Kenyan workloads, and the ease of building something more complicated than you need.

AWS offers a service for everything, which is genuinely useful and quietly dangerous. Architectures grow more elaborate than the problem requires because each additional service is easy to add and the bill arrives a month later.

Cost behaviour is the part people design around last. Charges for data leaving the platform and between availability zones accumulate in ways that do not appear in a proof of concept and become significant in production.

For Kenyan workloads there is also no local region, so latency to European or Middle Eastern regions is a real design input rather than a footnote. For most business applications it is acceptable and worth knowing before rather than after.

We size the monthly bill before building rather than after. An architecture that looks elegant and costs four times the estimate is a failure regardless of how well it runs, and clients rarely get that number from a supplier being paid to build it.

Frequently asked questions about AWS

Why do AWS bills grow unexpectedly?

Data transfer mostly. Charges for data leaving the platform and crossing zones are invisible in a small test and material at production volume.

Do you estimate cost before building?

Yes, and clients rarely get that figure from a supplier being paid to build. An architecture costing four times the estimate is a failure however well it runs.

Is there an AWS region in Kenya?

No, so workloads run from other regions and round trip time is a real design input rather than a footnote to discover later.

Does that latency matter?

For most business applications it is acceptable. For anything latency sensitive it needs designing around, and knowing beforehand is the point.

Why prefer simpler architectures?

Because elaborate is easy to build and the operational burden lands on you. A system your team can run is worth more than one that impresses.

Can you reduce our current bill?

Often. Unused resources from finished projects are common, and finding them frequently pays for the engagement.

What is right sizing?

Matching resources to actual usage rather than what was provisioned optimistically at the start, which is usually generous.

Should we use managed services?

Frequently yes. They cost more per unit and less in your people time, and that trade is usually worth making for a small team.

Can it connect AWS to our business systems?

Yes, and that is common where an application runs on AWS and the operational systems sit elsewhere.

How long does AWS work take?

Three to eight weeks depending on scope, with the cost estimate produced before the build rather than alongside it.

Do we need a dedicated cloud engineer?

For a simple architecture, usually not, which is one of the arguments for keeping it simple.

What about data residency?

Worth checking against your obligations before choosing a region, particularly for regulated data, and we would raise it early.

Can it handle high traffic?

Yes, and scaling is one of the genuine advantages, though it also means cost scales with traffic in ways worth modelling.

Who owns the account?

You do. We work in your account rather than ours, so nothing is hostage to a supplier relationship.

Can our team take it over?

Yes, and it is documented for that. Infrastructure only we understand is a dependency you did not sign up for.

What if we want to move off AWS later?

Simpler architectures move more easily. Heavy use of platform specific managed services makes leaving harder, which is worth weighing upfront.

Do you set up cost alerts?

Yes, as standard, since a bill trend visible weekly is manageable and the same trend at month end is a surprise.

Can it host AI workloads?

Yes, and those particularly need cost modelling since usage based charges scale with success rather than with headcount.

Who can access our AWS account?

Credentials you issue with the narrowest permissions that work, revocable by you at any time.

What does AWS work cost?

Scope determines the build, quoted alongside an estimated monthly running cost so both numbers are visible.

Glossary of terms

AWS terms in plain language.

Data transfer cost
Charges for data leaving the platform or crossing zones, which surprise people most often.
Region
The geographic location of infrastructure, which matters here since there is no local Kenyan region.
Availability zone
A separate facility within a region, where crossing between them carries a charge.
Managed service
A service AWS operates for you, which costs more per unit and less in your time.
Right sizing
Matching resources to actual usage rather than to what was provisioned optimistically.
Orphaned resource
Infrastructure still running and still billed for a project that ended.
